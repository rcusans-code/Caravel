The opportunity

Billions in tokenized funds earn yield but can't raise cash.

Stellar already holds some of the safest assets in finance: tokenized Treasury and money-market funds. Holders who need cash today must redeem and wait. Almost none of these assets can be borrowed against.

Our approach

The assets are safe. Getting cash out is slow.

Every dollar token on Stellar is ultimately backed by short-dated US Treasuries. The risk is not the asset itself. It is access: issuers can freeze tokens, only approved holders can redeem, and redemptions settle on business days only.

Where the risk sits: freezes, approved-holder rules, business-day redemption.

So lending against them should work the way bank repo desks have for decades: a fixed term, a daily price check and time to top up, not an automatic sell-off triggered in seconds.

Today

Floating-rate lending pools

  • Needs a live price every few seconds for an asset priced once a day
  • Can force a sale on an outdated or missing price
  • Seized fund shares can't be sold outside business hours
  • Floating rate, no end date
With Caravel

Fixed-term repo

  • Priced at the start, at each daily check and at maturity
  • No margin call on an outdated price
  • A margin call and time to top up always come before default
  • Fixed rate, fixed term, one amount due
How it works

Four steps, from offer to repayment.

In the demo, one day runs in 60 seconds
  1. 01 · Offer The lender sets the terms Amount, term, rate and accepted collateral. The lender's cash waits in escrow. The collateral discount is set per asset, not per offer.
  2. 02 · Accept The borrower accepts The borrower posts fund shares worth more than the loan. Collateral goes in and cash comes out in a single transaction.
  3. 03 · Monitor Daily price check The fund's NAV is checked every day. If coverage falls below the threshold, the borrower gets time to add collateral.
  4. 04 · Close Repay or default One amount due, fixed on day one. Repay any time. If it is still unpaid after maturity and a grace period, an approved buyer purchases the collateral.

Who it is for

Built for active treasury and trading desks.

Borrowing against Treasuries makes sense when you can earn more on the cash than the loan costs. That is why Caravel is built for trading firms, market makers and treasury teams, not for adding leverage to a passive position.

Borrowers
Raise cash for a set term at a known cost while keeping your Treasury fund position. No redemption, no waiting for the next business day.
Lenders
Earn a fixed rate, quoted as a spread over the Treasury yield. Protected by the haircut, margin calls and an approved buyer. You are always repaid in USDC.
Approved buyers
Issuer-approved market makers buy collateral from defaulted loans at a discount and redeem it at NAV. Aave Horizon uses the same model.
Fund issuers
Their fund becomes eligible collateral, and they keep full control over who may hold it.

Why Stellar

The assets are already here. So is the infrastructure.

Any issuer's rules
Caravel only uses the standard token interface. Each issuer approves the escrow its own way: authorized trustlines for Franklin Templeton's BENJI, Spiko's own permission contracts for its funds.
Native tokens
Circle's USDC and fund tokens are used directly, with no wrapping or bridging.
Delivery versus payment
Collateral and cash change hands in a single transaction.
Standard price feeds
One standard interface for fund prices. Switching providers is a setting, not a rebuild.
Fast, low-cost settlement
Final in seconds for a fraction of a cent, so daily checks cost almost nothing.
Collateral

Collateral discounts based on real risk, not labels.

Each asset has a minimum haircut set in the contract. Lenders can't go below it, and an asset's haircut can change without redeploying anything.

Risk, stated plainly

The risks, in plain terms.

  • A narrow safety margin. A 3% haircut minus the 2% buyer discount leaves about 1% of cushion. If the fund's NAV falls further before the collateral is sold, the lender absorbs the shortfall, and it is recorded publicly. A reserve funded by fees is planned.
  • Outdated prices pause risky actions, never repayment. Each asset has its own freshness window, about four days for funds that don't price at weekends, so a Friday price still counts on Sunday. Past it, new loans, margin calls and collateral sales pause. Repaying and adding collateral always work.
  • Issuers keep control. On mainnet, each issuer must approve the escrow and every buyer, and some issuers can pause or change their tokens.
  • Built for active capital. The loan rate is higher than the fund's own yield, so it suits firms that can earn more with the cash elsewhere.

FAQ

Questions, answered simply.

What Caravel is, how a loan works, and what protects each side.

Still have a question? Write to us.

What is Caravel?

A lending service for tokenized Treasury funds on Stellar. Fund holders borrow US dollars (USDC) against their fund shares, at a fixed rate for a fixed term. Lenders earn that fixed rate.

How does a loan work?

A lender posts an offer: amount, term and rate. A borrower accepts it and deposits fund shares worth a little more than the loan. The shares go into escrow and the cash goes to the borrower in the same transaction. At maturity, the borrower repays the agreed amount and gets the shares back.

Do I have to sell or redeem my fund shares?

No. Your shares stay in escrow during the loan and keep earning the fund's yield. You get cash today without redeeming and waiting for the next business day.

What can I use as collateral?

The demo uses tBENJI, a test version of Franklin Templeton's BENJI fund. We plan to start with the Treasury funds of Spiko and Franklin Templeton, the two largest issuers on Stellar, then add other assets with larger haircuts. See the collateral table above.

What is a haircut?

A safety discount on the collateral. With a 3% haircut, $1,000 of fund shares supports a loan of up to $970. It protects the lender if the fund's price falls.

What happens if the fund's price falls?

The fund's price (NAV) is checked every day. If the collateral is no longer worth enough, the borrower gets a margin call and time to add collateral or repay. Nothing is sold automatically in that moment.

What happens at weekends, when funds don't publish a price?

Each asset has its own price freshness window, about four days for funds that publish NAV on business days only. A Friday price still counts on Saturday and Sunday, so loans can open and margin can be checked as usual. Repaying and adding collateral never wait for a price.

What if the borrower doesn't repay?

After maturity and a short grace period, an issuer-approved buyer purchases the collateral at 2% below NAV. The lender is paid the amount due, and anything left over goes back to the borrower.

Why a fixed term instead of an open-ended loan?

Tokenized funds price once a day and redeem on business days. A fixed term with daily checks matches how these assets actually work, and both sides know the rate and the amount due from day one.

Who can borrow and lend?

Fund tokens can only be held by wallets the issuer has approved, so borrowers are already approved holders. Lenders provide USDC and are always repaid in USDC. On mainnet, issuers also approve the escrow and the buyers.

What does it cost?

Borrowers pay the fixed interest set in the offer. Caravel takes a protocol fee from that interest. Network fees on Stellar are under a cent per transaction.

Is Caravel live?

It runs on Stellar testnet today and the contracts have not been audited yet. We are preparing a mainnet pilot with fund issuers. You can request access below.
